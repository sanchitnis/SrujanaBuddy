## S2-01: Achiever / AI-Technology — Internship targeting

**Persona**: Arya (Achiever / CP-1 AI-Technology) — now at Stage 2 entry
**Stage**: 2 — Application

**Student Input**:
> I've finished two ML courses and built a sentiment analysis project on GitHub. I want to apply for AI internships. Which companies should I target and how should I prepare?

**Expected coaching behaviors**:
1. Validates the Stage 1 completion evidence (courses + GitHub project) before prescribing next steps.
2. Helps her assess readiness honestly — asks about depth, not just completion ("can you explain the attention mechanism without notes?").
3. Recommends specific, realistic targets based on her profile (not just "apply to Google and Microsoft").
4. Gives a concrete preparation checklist: portfolio review, DSA basics, ML interview patterns, communication.
5. Links to Stage 2 milestone in [`references/ai-engineer-pathways.md`](../../references/ai-engineer-pathways.md) for her pathway.
6. Next action is specific: one application, one preparation task, one portfolio improvement.

**Quality signals**:
- [ ] Stage 1 evidence acknowledged before Stage 2 prescription
- [ ] Depth-check question asked (not just "do you have a project?")
- [ ] Company targets are realistic for current profile (not aspirational reach-only)
- [ ] Preparation checklist covers portfolio, DSA, and communication — not just coding
- [ ] AI pathway internship readiness milestone referenced
- [ ] Next action specific: one application + one prep task

**Common failure modes**:
- F-1: Prescribing Stage 3 advice (research internship, open-source leadership) without confirming Stage 2 readiness
- F-9: Giving generic "apply to tier-1 companies" without considering her actual profile
- F-6: Overcautious ("you need 6 more months of preparation") when she has genuine Stage 2 readiness

## S2-02: Explorer / Entrepreneurship — First real-world experience

**Persona**: Persona 2 — Rohan (Explorer / CP-3 Entrepreneurship)
**Stage**: 2 — Application

**Student Input**:
> I did a 2-month internship at a solar energy startup. I mostly helped with marketing and some basic data analysis. I don't know if this counts as anything useful. I'm still not sure whether I want to do edtech or sustainability.

**Expected coaching behaviors**:
1. Names the internship as genuine Stage 2 evidence — validates it before he dismisses it.
2. Helps him extract the competencies demonstrated: data analysis, marketing in a startup context, working under real constraints.
3. Does NOT force a choice between edtech and sustainability — helps him see both as testable hypotheses.
4. Suggests what he should build next to test one of the paths with low commitment.
5. Evidence artifact: one reflection document capturing what he learned from the internship (even informally).
6. Does not produce a full business plan or startup roadmap — too early.

**Quality signals**:
- [ ] Internship named as real Stage 2 evidence before any redirection
- [ ] Specific competencies extracted from the experience (not generic "you learned a lot")
- [ ] No forced choice between edtech and sustainability
- [ ] Low-commitment test suggested for one path
- [ ] Evidence artifact assigned: reflection or learning debrief document
- [ ] No startup roadmap produced in this session

**Common failure modes**:
- F-3: Ignoring the internship evidence and focusing on the career uncertainty
- F-2: Treating Rohan like an Achiever and pushing toward immediate startup formation
- F-1: Treating him as Stage 3-ready (Creation) before Stage 2 competencies are consolidated

## S2-03: Passenger / Design-Creative — Aspiration surfaces, first external step

**Persona**: Persona 7 — Nanditha (Passenger / CP-4 Design-Creative)
**Stage**: 1→2 transition — aspiration just surfaced

**Student Input**:
> Actually… I've been doing some designs on Figma on my own. Just for fun. No one has seen them. I don't know if they're good enough to show anyone.

**Expected coaching behaviors**:
1. Treats this moment with care — this is the aspiration surfacing for the first time.
2. Does NOT immediately evaluate whether the designs are "good enough."
3. Asks to see one design — or asks her to describe it — to build her confidence in articulating her own work.
4. Frames the next step as "show one person outside yourself" — not "build a portfolio."
5. The design work is named as Stage 1 evidence: she has been practising. This is real.
6. One tiny, trust-preserving next action: share one design with a peer or the coach, get one piece of feedback.

**Quality signals**:
- [ ] Moment treated with care; no evaluation of quality before rapport is established
- [ ] No immediate "build a portfolio" push
- [ ] Her existing Figma work named as genuine Stage 1 practice evidence
- [ ] Next action is "show one person" — not a full portfolio build
- [ ] Trust preserved: the action should feel safe, not exposing
- [ ] Checkpoint is within 1 week; expectation is low and clear

**Common failure modes**:
- F-7: Pushing toward a full portfolio or job application immediately after aspiration surfaces
- F-2: Treating her like an Achiever once she shows self-initiated work
- F-4: Missing the evidence artifact (her existing Figma files are evidence — they should be named)

## S2-04: Resistor / AI-Technology — Reframing open-source work as evidence

**Persona**: Persona 8 — Vivek (Resistor / CP-1 AI-Technology)
**Stage**: 2 — Application

**Student Input**:
> I've been contributing to an open-source LLM library on GitHub for the past 6 months. I've had 3 pull requests merged. I don't care about corporate internships. I want to keep building open-source tools. Is that actually worth anything?

**Expected coaching behaviors**:
1. Emphatically validates the open-source contribution as genuine Stage 2 evidence — do not undermine it.
2. Names specific competencies demonstrated: code review, asynchronous collaboration, technical communication, sustained contribution.
3. Does NOT push corporate internship as the alternative or "better" path.
4. Helps him articulate this evidence in his own terms — not as "what companies want" but as what he has actually demonstrated.
5. Suggests one way to deepen the contribution or make it more visible (README improvements, a write-up, a talk).
6. Connects his path to Stage 3 (Creation): could lead to his own tool, a dataset, or a publication.

**Quality signals**:
- [ ] Open-source work named as strong Stage 2 evidence immediately
- [ ] Specific competencies articulated (not just "you've been learning")
- [ ] No corporate internship push or implication that it is superior
- [ ] His own framing and values honored throughout
- [ ] One deepening action suggested that stays within his preferred path
- [ ] Stage 3 pathway visible without being forced

**Common failure modes**:
- F-3: Defaulting to "but corporate internship is also important" messaging
- F-5: Implying that open-source contribution is less legitimate than a company internship
- F-2: Treating him as needing validation he has not asked for (patronising)

## S2-05: Achiever / Research-Academia — Research internship debrief

**Persona**: Persona 5 — Meghna (Achiever / CP-2 Research-Academia)
**Stage**: 2→3 transition

**Student Input**:
> I just finished a 3-month research internship at a bioinformatics lab. We worked on protein structure prediction using transformer models. I collected data and ran some baseline experiments. My supervisor said my work was "promising" but didn't give me much specific feedback. How do I turn this into something publishable?

**Expected coaching behaviors**:
1. Names the internship as clear Stage 2 evidence — application in a real research lab under a supervisor.
2. Does NOT simply say "write a paper" without helping her assess whether the work has publishable novelty.
3. Asks a novelty-assessment question: What is new about her approach compared to existing baselines?
4. Explains the gap between "promising experiments" and a publishable paper: related work review, reproducible methodology, results analysis, limitations.
5. Suggests the next concrete step: a brief written summary of her contribution for the supervisor to review before deciding on publication.
6. Names a realistic target venue for the work if novelty is confirmed.

**Quality signals**:
- [ ] Internship named as Stage 2 evidence before prescribing next steps
- [ ] Novelty question asked before "write a paper" is prescribed
- [ ] Gap between promising experiments and publication is explained clearly
- [ ] Next action is written (contribution summary for supervisor) — not verbal
- [ ] Realistic venue suggested (not just "aim for NeurIPS" without context)
- [ ] Stage 3 (Creation) transition is visible but not yet assumed complete

**Common failure modes**:
- F-9: Prescribing a specific venue without asking about the novelty of the work
- F-1: Treating her as already Stage 3 (paper-ready) without the novelty check
- F-6: Being so cautious ("your work might not be publishable") that she loses momentum
