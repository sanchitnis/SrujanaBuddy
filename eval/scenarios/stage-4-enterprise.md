# Stage 4 — Enterprise: Test Scenarios

## How to use

Run each scenario by pasting the **Student Input** verbatim into a fresh SrujanaBuddy session.
Evaluate the response against the **Quality Signals**. Mark each as PASS / FAIL / PARTIAL.
Log results in [`eval/data/eval-log-template.md`](../data/eval-log-template.md).

Scenarios in this file cover all three Stage 4 tracks:
- **Research track** (patents and publications)
- **Venture track** (startups and market validation)
- **Career Enterprise track** (systemic contribution in a role)

## S4-01: Achiever / AI-Technology — Research Track: First top-tier conference submission

**Persona**: Arya (Achiever / CP-1 AI-Technology)
**Stage**: 4 — Enterprise (Research track)

**Student Input**:
> I've finished my RAG evaluation paper. My faculty mentor reviewed it and said it's ready. I'm going to submit to EMNLP. The deadline is in 10 days. What do I need to check before I submit?

**Expected coaching behaviors**:
1. Validates the milestone: faculty-reviewed paper ready for submission to a top-tier NLP venue.
2. Provides a concrete pre-submission checklist: abstract, contribution clarity, related work completeness, experiments reproducible, limitations section present, references formatted correctly, camera-ready metadata (author list, institution, anonymization if required).
3. Checks whether she has posted a preprint on arXiv — if yes, ensures this is declared in the submission system; if not, discusses the option.
4. Reminds her of the anonymization requirement for double-blind review (EMNLP is double-blind — no author names/affiliations in the paper itself).
5. Names the evidence artifact: submission confirmation with assigned paper ID.
6. Sets a post-submission checkpoint: when reviews are expected and what to do when they arrive.

**Quality signals**:
- [ ] Pre-submission checklist is specific and covers anonymization, references, limitations section
- [ ] EMNLP double-blind requirement mentioned
- [ ] Preprint strategy discussed (arXiv timing relative to submission)
- [ ] Evidence artifact named: submission confirmation + paper ID
- [ ] Post-submission checkpoint set (review timeline, rebuttal preparation)
- [ ] Coaching matches her pace — no slowing down

**Common failure modes**:
- F-9: Not knowing EMNLP is double-blind; not catching author information in the paper
- F-9: Incorrect advice on arXiv timing relative to double-blind submission
- F-6: Excessive caution ("maybe submit to a workshop first") when she has faculty approval and is ready

## S4-02: Achiever / Research-Academia — Research Track: Patent filing decision

**Persona**: Persona 5 — Meghna (Achiever / CP-2 Research-Academia)
**Stage**: 4 — Enterprise (Research track — Patent)

**Student Input**:
> I spoke to REVA's IPR cell. They think my protein folding method is patentable. They've asked me to write an invention disclosure. I've never done this before. What should I include in it?

**Expected coaching behaviors**:
1. Validates the engagement with REVA IPR cell as the correct first step.
2. Gives specific guidance on what an invention disclosure must contain: title, inventors (all who contributed inventively), problem being solved, prior solutions and their limitations, description of the invention, how it works (technical description), embodiments and variations, potential use cases, whether it has been publicly disclosed.
3. Advises on the public disclosure question: if she has presented this at a conference or posted on arXiv, she must declare this — it affects priority date.
4. Recommends attaching her experimental data and any drawings or diagrams.
5. Does NOT write the disclosure for her — coaching is to enable her to write it.
6. Evidence artifact: completed invention disclosure submitted to REVA IPR cell.

**Quality signals**:
- [ ] Engagement with IPR cell validated as correct first step
- [ ] Invention disclosure contents listed specifically (title, inventors, problem, prior art, description, use cases)
- [ ] Public disclosure question raised explicitly
- [ ] Diagrams/experimental data attachment recommended
- [ ] Coaching enables her to write it; does not produce a draft disclosure
- [ ] Evidence artifact: completed and submitted invention disclosure

**Common failure modes**:
- F-9: Not knowing what an invention disclosure contains
- F-9: Missing the public disclosure question (critical for priority date)
- F-6: Being so vague ("just write what it does") that the output will be unusable

## S4-03: Explorer / Entrepreneurship — Venture Track: Market validation gap

**Persona**: Persona 9 — Priya (Achiever / CP-3 Entrepreneurship) — but also applicable to Persona 2 — Rohan
**Stage**: 4 — Enterprise (Venture track)

**Student Input**:
> We've been at this for 6 months. We have an MVP. We have a pitch deck. We presented at two college fests. We got a lot of positive feedback. But we haven't gotten any actual users yet. REVA NEST has invited us to apply for their incubation program. Should we apply?

**Expected coaching behaviors**:
1. Names what she has — MVP, pitch deck, early exposure — as genuine Stage 3/Stage 4 entry evidence.
2. Flags the critical gap directly: "positive feedback at college fests" is not market validation. Real validation requires users who chose to use it, not audiences who applauded.
3. Does NOT say "yes, apply" without the validation gap being addressed.
4. Recommends a minimum validation sprint before the REVA NEST application: 10–15 user interviews with potential real users (not peers), and/or 3–5 pilot users who use the MVP in their actual context.
5. Explains what REVA NEST will look for: traction evidence, user feedback, problem-solution fit data.
6. REVA NEST application as the Stage 4 milestone — but only after validation evidence.

**Quality signals**:
- [ ] Current evidence named and valued accurately (Stage 3–4 entry)
- [ ] Validation gap named directly: college fest feedback is not user validation
- [ ] "Apply now" NOT recommended without a validation sprint
- [ ] Minimum validation sprint specified (10-15 interviews or 3-5 pilots)
- [ ] REVA NEST expectations explained: traction, problem-solution fit data
- [ ] REVA NEST application positioned as Stage 4 milestone after sprint

**Common failure modes**:
- F-1: Treating college fest positive feedback as real market validation (Stage 4-ready)
- F-3: Ignoring the validation gap and answering only the REVA NEST application question
- F-9: Not knowing what REVA NEST or incubation programs look for in early-stage applicants

## S4-04: Resistor / Social Enterprise — Venture Track: Impact documentation

**Persona**: Persona 4 — Kiran (Resistor / CP-6 Social Enterprise)
**Stage**: 4 — Enterprise (Venture track — social)

**Student Input**:
> Our water access tool is now being used in 7 villages. The local government has asked us to present the data at a district water management meeting. I want to apply for a social impact grant. What should I document before I go into that meeting?

**Expected coaching behaviors**:
1. Names this as clear Stage 4 evidence: tool deployed at scale, government stakeholder engagement, systemic impact visible.
2. Provides a concrete documentation checklist for the meeting: scope (number of villages, households, data points), outcomes (what decisions have been made using the data), adoption evidence (who is using it and how often), technical reliability, limitations and what is not covered.
3. Connects the documentation to the grant application: what social impact grants look for (problem statement, evidence of impact, scalability, team capacity, sustainability plan).
4. Does NOT reframe this as a startup or commercial opportunity.
5. Names grant sources relevant to civic tech and water access in Karnataka: DST-NIDHI, India Water Portal grants, Karnataka government's innovation schemes, NABARD rural innovation funds.
6. Evidence artifact: one-page impact brief ready before the meeting.

**Quality signals**:
- [ ] Stage 4 evidence named explicitly (deployment at scale, government engagement)
- [ ] Meeting documentation checklist is specific (scope, outcomes, adoption evidence, limitations)
- [ ] Grant application requirements connected to the meeting documentation
- [ ] No startup or commercial reframing
- [ ] At least two relevant grant sources named (ideally Karnataka/water-specific)
- [ ] Evidence artifact: one-page impact brief

**Common failure modes**:
- F-3: Reframing this as a startup opportunity and recommending REVA NEST instead
- F-9: Generic grant advice without Karnataka-specific or water sector-specific options
- F-5: Implicitly treating civic tech impact as less legitimate than commercial product impact

## S4-05: Achiever / AI-Technology — Career Enterprise Track: First high-impact role

**Persona**: Modified Arya — has accepted a role at an AI research lab (not a startup, not academia)
**Stage**: 4 — Enterprise (Career Enterprise track)

**Student Input**:
> I've accepted an offer at an AI research lab. I start in 3 months. I want to make a real impact in the first year — not just be another new hire. What should I be thinking about before I start?

**Expected coaching behaviors**:
1. Validates the role as a Stage 4 expression — a research lab role is enterprise.
2. Distinguishes between "getting things done" and "creating systemic impact" — she asked for the latter.
3. Pre-joining checklist: read recent publications from the team she is joining, identify one gap or open question in their work, reach out to a team member before day 1 to learn what problems are live.
4. First 90 days guidance: listen-first orientation, find one concrete contribution opportunity early (a tool improvement, a code review, a documentation gap), establish a research rhythm.
5. Evidence artifacts for the first year: one shipped contribution (code, paper draft, tool), one person mentored or helped, one internal talk given.
6. Reminds her of the Career Enterprise threshold: impact visible beyond the immediate team is the Stage 4 signal.

**Quality signals**:
- [ ] Role named as Stage 4 enterprise expression
- [ ] "Systemic impact" vs. "getting things done" distinction made
- [ ] Pre-joining actions specified (reading, identifying gaps, reaching out)
- [ ] First 90 days guidance: listen-first, then one concrete early contribution
- [ ] Three evidence artifacts named for Year 1
- [ ] Career Enterprise threshold stated: impact visible beyond immediate team

**Common failure modes**:
- F-1: Treating job entry as Stage 2 or 3 when she is at Stage 4
- F-6: Generic advice ("be proactive and learn from others") without specific actions
- F-3: Focusing only on technical preparation and ignoring the systemic contribution dimension
