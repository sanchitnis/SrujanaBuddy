# Five Student Spheres Framework
## Integral Balance Model for REVA Students

## Why this model exists

Students do not struggle in one area only. Academic pressure, attention fragmentation,
identity shifts, relationship stress, and energy depletion interact every week.
The Five Student Spheres model keeps coaching whole-person and action-oriented.

This framework is the default structure for:
1. Weekly review.
2. Balance coaching.
3. Profile trend tracking.
4. Recovery planning.

## The five spheres

### 1. Shiksha (Learning)
Covers academics, subject mastery, revision quality, assignment execution, and self-learning.

Signals of health:
1. Runway starts early for assignments and exams.
2. Concept understanding improves over time.
3. Student can explain what they learned in their own words.

Signals of risk:
1. Last-minute work pattern.
2. Memorization without understanding.
3. AI output dependence without explain-back.

### 2. Antarmana (Inner Life)
Covers emotional state, self-awareness, discipline, values, motivation quality, and dopamine ecology.

Signals of health:
1. Stable emotional recovery after stress spikes.
2. Commitments are realistic and completed consistently.
3. Attention is mostly intentional, not impulsive.

Signals of risk:
1. Chronic anxiety or shutdown.
2. Urgency addiction and distraction loops.
3. Harsh self-talk and collapsing self-trust.

### 3. Sangha (Relationships)
Covers peers, family, mentors, teamwork, communication quality, and conflict handling.

Signals of health:
1. Student asks for help early when blocked.
2. Team contribution is reliable.
3. Mentors and peers are used as growth supports.

Signals of risk:
1. Isolation during stress cycles.
2. Recurring unresolved team conflict.
3. Communication avoidance and missed follow-through.

### 4. Seva (Contribution)
Covers contribution through clubs, NCC, NSS, social projects, open source,
and campus or community impact.

Signals of health:
1. Student contributes beyond attendance.
2. Responsibilities evolve from participant to organizer.
3. Contribution outcomes are converted to portfolio evidence.

Signals of risk:
1. Contribution only when convenient.
2. No continuity across semesters.
3. High effort with no artifact or impact record.

### 5. Sharira (Body and Energy)
Covers sleep, food quality, movement, energy management, and recovery.

Signals of health:
1. Sleep rhythm supports learning.
2. Energy is sustained across most of the day.
3. Student has a realistic recovery routine.

Signals of risk:
1. Sleep debt and cognitive fog.
2. Stimulant-dependent study cycles.
3. No movement and burnout accumulation.

## Weekly review protocol

Every weekly review must include:
1. One status line per sphere.
2. One risk per sphere.
3. One stabilizing action per sphere.
4. One cross-sphere insight.

Cross-sphere insight rule:
A block in one sphere is often resolved by action in another sphere.
Example: Shiksha collapse may need Sharira and Antarmana stabilization first.

## Scoring template

Use a 1-10 self-rating each week.

| Sphere | Score | This week win | This week risk | Next stabilizing action |
|--------|-------|---------------|----------------|--------------------------|
| Shiksha | /10 | | | |

| Sangha | /10 | | | |
| Seva | /10 | | | |
| Sharira | /10 | | | |

## Non-negotiable balancing rules

1. No sphere should stay below 5 for more than two consecutive weekly reviews.
2. Core academic stability is protected before optional load expansion.
3. If Sharira drops, reduce optional commitments before adding new goals.
4. If Antarmana is unstable, shift to grounding and minimum viable commitments.
5. Every Seva activity should generate one evidence artifact for portfolio.

## Year-group emphasis hints

1. Year 1: prioritize Shiksha + Sangha + Sharira stabilization.
2. Year 2-3: scale Seva and skill depth while protecting Shiksha runway.
3. Final Year: prioritize Shiksha + career transition with Antarmana regulation.
4. PG: balance research depth with Sharira and Sangha continuity.

## Coaching close format

For sphere-based sessions, close with:
1. Next step now.
2. One time-bound commitment.
3. Checkpoint date and evidence.
