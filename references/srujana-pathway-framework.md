# Srujana Pathway Framework
## Stage-wise Coaching and Evidence Progression

This framework defines the four Srujana stages and how coaching maps
aspiration to measurable capability and portfolio evidence.

## Stage model

### Stage 1: Foundation (Knowledge, Skill, Attitude, Ability)
Primary sources:
1. Curriculum learning.
2. Clubs and campus participation.
3. Guided self-learning.

Expected outputs:
1. Basic competency demonstrations.
2. Study and execution discipline.
3. Early artifacts (mini projects, reports, reflections).

### Stage 2: Application (Internships and industry-mentored projects)
Primary sources:
1. Internship readiness and participation.
2. Applied projects with external relevance.
3. Team collaboration under deadlines.

Expected outputs:
1. Applied problem-solving evidence.
2. Mentor feedback records.
3. Improved communication and delivery quality.

### Stage 3: Creation (Product, solution, consulting, research)
Primary sources:
1. Product or solution design.
2. Research and advanced project execution.
3. Iterative validation with users/stakeholders.

Expected outputs:
1. Working prototypes or validated concepts.
2. Research notes or submissions.
3. Integration-level competency evidence.

### Stage 4: Enterprise (Enterprising careers, startups, and high-impact research)
Primary sources:
1. Venture-oriented execution.
2. Market and value proposition validation.
3. Team and execution rhythm under uncertainty.
4. Impactful research — patents, publications, and field contributions.
5. Systemic career contribution beyond the immediate job scope.

Expected outputs:
1. MVP progress evidence (venture track).
2. Validation artifacts — user interviews, pilot results, pivot rationale (venture track).
3. Filed patent application or granted patent (research track — invention).
4. Accepted or submitted peer-reviewed paper (research track — publication).
5. Career-level systemic contribution evidence (career enterprise track).

#### Stage 4 — Research Track: Patents and Publications

For students pursuing research careers, graduate study, or positions at research-intensive organisations.

**Patent pathway**

Novelty criteria — the invention must satisfy all four:
- Solves a problem that existing solutions do not adequately address.
- Non-obvious to a skilled practitioner in the field.
- Technically implementable, not a vague idea.
- Prior art searched and novelty claim defensible.

Process:
1. Invention disclosure — 2–3 pages: what the invention is, what problem it solves, how it differs from prior art, who would use it.
2. Prior art search — Google Patents, USPTO, EPO Espacenet, arXiv, ACM DL, IEEE Xplore; minimum 15 references examined and distinguished.
3. REVA IPR collaboration — engage REVA's technology transfer / IPR cell for institutional support and co-filing.
4. Provisional filing — establishes priority date; 12-month window for complete specification.
5. Complete specification — full application with claims, drawings, and abstract.
6. Examination and grant — respond to examination queries; granted patent is the completion evidence.

Evidence ladder:

| Level | Evidence |
|-------|----------|
| Minimum | Filed application receipt number + invention disclosure reviewed by domain expert |
| Strong | Published patent application with assigned publication number |
| Full Stage 4 | Granted patent (Indian Patent Office, USPTO, EPO, or equivalent) |
| Distinguished | Granted patent + licensing conversation or technology transfer initiated |

Resources: ipindia.gov.in · uspto.gov · worldwide.espacenet.com · wipo.int/pct

---

**Publication pathway**

Venue ladder:

| Level | Venue type | Signal |
|-------|-----------|--------|
| L1 | Internal REVA symposium / department research day | Early practice; not external peer review |
| L2 | Workshop at a recognised conference | First external peer review experience |
| L3 | Full paper at ACM / IEEE / Springer conference | Stage 3–4 boundary evidence |
| L4 | Top-tier conference (NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR, ACL, CVPR, USENIX, KDD) | Strong Stage 4 signal |
| L5 | Journal paper — Q1/Q2 Scopus or Web of Science indexed | Full Stage 4 research enterprise evidence |
| L6 | Journal with demonstrable field impact — citations, dataset adoption | Distinguished Stage 4 |

Venue selection by field:
- AI/ML: NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR, AAAI, ACL, EMNLP, CVPR, ICCV, IEEE TPAMI, JMLR
- Data Science: KDD, SIGMOD, VLDB, ICDE
- Security: IEEE S&P, CCS, USENIX Security, NDSS
- Interdisciplinary: Nature Machine Intelligence, Science Advances, PNAS

Paper process:
1. Research question — a precise, answerable question; not a topic.
2. Literature review — minimum 20 relevant papers read, synthesised, gaps identified.
3. Methodology — a replicable experimental or analytical approach.
4. Execution — data collection, experiments, analysis, results.
5. Writing — abstract, introduction, related work, method, experiments, results, discussion, limitations, conclusion, references.
6. Internal review — share with at least one faculty mentor and one peer before submission.
7. Venue selection — consider topic fit, acceptance rate, and indexing.
8. Submission and revision — submit, receive reviews, write a thorough rebuttal or revision.
9. Camera-ready and publication — final submission with DOI or proceedings link.

Evidence ladder:

| Level | Evidence |
|-------|----------|
| Minimum | Submission confirmation + literature review (20+ papers synthesised) |
| Strong | Reviewer feedback received and revision prepared |
| Full Stage 4 | Accepted and published at L3+ venue with DOI or proceedings link |
| Distinguished | Accepted at L4+ venue; or paper with documented citations or adoption |

Resources: overleaf.com · arxiv.org · paperswithcode.com · Semantic Scholar · Connected Papers

Coaching notes:
- Rejection is not failure — receiving expert reviews is meaningful Stage 3–4 evidence.
- Resubmission after revision is itself a Stage 4 competency; coach students to persist.
- Co-authored papers with faculty are valid — document the student's specific contribution.
- A filed patent combined with a paper on the same invention is a particularly strong portfolio signal.
- Point students to REVA research funding schemes and conference travel support.

---

**Dataset, benchmark, or open-source contribution of field significance**

Evidence criteria:
- Addresses a gap the research community has not filled.
- Released publicly (HuggingFace Datasets, Zenodo, GitHub) with a data card or model card.
- Adopted by at least one group outside the student's team (citations, forks, or direct use).

---

#### Stage 4 — Venture Track: Startups and Market Validation

Primary sources:
1. Venture formation — company registered or student venture formally initiated.
2. Problem-solution fit validated (user interviews, prototype feedback, surveys).
3. Product-market fit evidence (paying users, active users, letters of intent, pilots).
4. Fundraising or grant: seed investment, Startup India, iCreate, REVA incubator, DST-NIDHI.
5. Team rhythm — co-founders recruited, roles defined, execution cadence established.

Coaching notes:
- A student who starts a company and fails has more Stage 4 evidence than one who only plans.
- Pivot decisions are enterprise evidence — explain what was learned and why direction changed.
- Use institutional support: REVA incubation cell, Startup India registration, iCreate.

---

#### Stage 4 — Career Enterprise Track: Systemic Roles and High-Impact Placement

Expected outputs:
1. Role at an organisation where work creates systemic value (AI lab, policy body, high-growth startup, social enterprise).
2. Demonstrated impact within the role (shipped product, measurable outcome, team led or mentored).
3. Ongoing contribution beyond the job description — mentoring juniors, open-source, public writing, or events.

## Stage transition rules

1. Progression is readiness-based, not semester-gated.
2. Students may complete any subset of stages.
3. Every stage must produce measurable competency evidence.
4. If academic risk rises, reduce high-intensity optional load first.

## Stage readiness signals

Evaluate readiness using:
1. Completion consistency.
2. Concept mastery trend.
3. Execution under deadlines.
4. Feedback integration quality.
5. Evidence quality in portfolio.

## Mandatory traceability chain

For each pathway action, preserve this chain:
Aspiration -> Stage -> Competency -> Action -> Evidence.

## Coaching output format for stage sessions

1. Current stage and readiness statement.
2. One stage-appropriate next action.
3. One evidence artifact to produce.
4. One checkpoint date.

## Stage 4 evidence quick-reference

| Track | Minimum evidence | Full Stage 4 signal |
|-------|-----------------|---------------------|
| Patent | Filed application receipt + invention disclosure reviewed | Granted patent or published application |
| Publication | Submitted paper + literature review (20+ papers) | Accepted paper at L3+ venue with DOI |
| Dataset / OSS | Public release with documentation | Adoption evidence — citations, forks, or users |
| Startup / Venture | MVP tested by real users | PMF evidence, investment, or sustained traction |
| Career Enterprise | High-impact role with measurable contribution | Systemic visible contribution beyond job scope |
