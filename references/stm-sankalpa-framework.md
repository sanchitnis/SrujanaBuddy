## Part 1 — The Sankalpa Tradition

### Origin and meaning

**Sankalpa** (संकल्प) is a Vedic concept meaning wholehearted intention or sacred commitment. From *sam* (complete, fully aligned) + *kalpa* (rule, intention). It appears in the Upanishads and Yoga traditions as the foundation of disciplined action.

In the Yoga Vasistha, Sankalpa is described as the very power by which the mind creates experience:
> *"This world is nothing but the will (sankalpa) of the mind."*

In ritual practice, a Sankalpa is stated before any significant act — a brief declaration of intent that anchors the practitioner in purpose before beginning. This ritualized commitment is not passive; it is the mental alignment of will, energy, and action.

### Sankalpa in coaching context

In STM, Sankalpa is reframed for student life:
- **Not aspirational ("I want to…")** — but **committal ("I will, starting now…")**
- **Not open-ended** — but **completion-bounded** ("I will solve 5 problems for 30 minutes")
- **Completable at all sizes** — a 2-minute action is a fully valid fulfilled sankalpa

This is the key insight: **the size of the sankalpa does not determine its worth. The completion does.**

## Part 2 — Modern Psychology Foundations

### Atomic Habits (James Clear, 2018)

Key principles integrated in STM:

**1. Identity-based habits**  
The goal is not to complete a task — it is to become the person who completes such tasks. Every fulfilled sankalpa is a "vote" for the identity: *"I am someone who completes what I start."*

**2. The 2-minute rule**  
Any new habit should be reducible to a version that takes 2 minutes. Applied in STM as the **Tatkala sankalpa** (instant commitment) and the **2-minute start test** for all Dainika sankalpas.

**3. Habit stacking**  
Attach a new tiny sankalpa to an existing anchor behaviour: *"After [existing habit], I will [new sankalpa]."* Used in STM's streak-building phase.

**4. Celebration and reward**  
Immediate celebration (not just milestone reward) wires in the habit faster. In STM, every completion — even a tiny sankalpa — is explicitly celebrated in the moment.

### Implementation Intentions (Peter Gollwitzer, 1999)

Research shows that framing a commitment as an **if-then** or **when-then** plan dramatically increases follow-through:
> *"When I [situation], I will [action] for [duration]."*

This structure is built into every Dainika sankalpa in STM. It removes decision friction — the student doesn't need to decide when to act, because the sankalpa already contains the trigger.

### Duolingo Streak Psychology

Duolingo's streak system works because:
1. **Loss aversion**: breaking a streak feels more painful than continuing it
2. **Visual chain**: the unbroken chain is itself a motivator
3. **Rescue mechanism**: streak freeze (Sadhana Shield in STM) prevents catastrophic dropout after a single miss

Critical addition in STM: the streak is not about perfection but about **identity continuity**. Even a rescued streak communicates: *"I chose to come back."*

### Self-Determination Theory (Deci & Ryan)

Habits and commitments stick when they are:
- **Autonomy-supportive**: student chooses the sankalpa, not assigned
- **Competence-building**: tiny wins are calibrated to current skill, not aspirational difficulty
- **Relatedness-anchored**: accountability to self (identity), with optional peer or coach check-in

STM addresses all three:
- Students own their sankalpas completely
- Tiny sankalpa design ensures constant small competence experiences
- Weekly Svadhyaya builds the relational bond with one's own future self

### Habit Loop (Charles Duhigg, "The Power of Habit")

Cue → Routine → Reward

In STM:
- **Cue**: Morning ritual (time-based) or implementation intention (situation-based)
- **Routine**: The sankalpa execution itself
- **Reward**: Immediate celebration + archive entry + streak update

## Part 3 — The Neuroscience of Tiny Wins

When you complete a task — even a tiny one — the brain releases a small amount of dopamine. This release:
1. Reinforces the neural pathway used during the task
2. Creates a mild motivational pull toward repeating the behaviour
3. Tags the action with a "this worked" memory signal

**Why tiny wins matter more than big ones (for habit formation):**
- Big wins are rare → infrequent dopamine → weak habit reinforcement
- Tiny wins are daily → frequent dopamine → strong habit loop
- The pattern: *tiny wins → daily dopamine → identity formation → big wins follow*

This is why Duolingo's smallest daily lesson (one minute) builds more users than an ambitious monthly study schedule.

## Part 5 — Coaching Use of This Framework

### Calibrating sankalpa size

At the start of any STM session, check the student's **energy and clarity state** (from coaching context in profile):

| Student state | Sankalpa calibration |
|-------------|---------------------|
| High energy + clear | 1 ambitious Dainika + 2 tiny = standard load |
| Moderate energy | 1 medium Dainika + 1 tiny = reduced load |
| Low energy / overwhelmed | 2 tiny sankalpas only — no big one today |
| Post-crisis / recovery | 1 Tatkala sankalpa (2 min) — just restart the chain |

### Sankalpa rescue conversation

When a student has broken a streak or not completed commitments:

1. **No guilt opening**: *"Streaks break sometimes, da. That's okay. What matters is we're here now."*
2. **Pattern probe**: What got in the way? (energy? clarity? the sankalpa was too big?)
3. **Resize**: Was the sankalpa too ambitious? Break it down.
4. **Sadhana Shield**: Offer the rescue if available — double-tiny tomorrow.
5. **Identity anchor**: *"Coming back after a break is itself a fulfilled sankalpa."*

### Integration with Dopamine Stewardship

From `references/dopamine-stewardship-student.md`: run the daily baseline check (energy/mood 1-10, stimulation risk) before setting sankalpas. If energy ≤ 4, reduce to tiny-only. If stimulation risk is high, include one Tatkala sankalpa that uses physical movement as the cue.

### Weekly Svadhyaya coaching close

Standard close for any STM session:
1. One sankalpa the student is committing to right now
2. Streak status update
3. Identity statement: *"I am someone who completes what I start."*
